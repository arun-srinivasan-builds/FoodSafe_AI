What is Food Recall?

Section 28(1) of FSS Act, 2006 stipulates that if a food business operator considers or has reasons to believe that a food which he has processed, manufactured or distributed is not in compliance with this Act, or the rules or regulations made thereunder, he shall immediately initiate procedures to withdraw the food in question from the market and consumers indicating reasons for its withdrawal and inform the competent authorities thereof.

Recall functions as an action to remove food products from market at any stage of the food chain, including that possessed by consumer, which may pose a threat to the public health or food that violate the Act, or the rules or regulations made thereunder.

FOOD RECALL PORTAL

Food Recall information and submissions are managed through the FoSCoS (Food Safety Compliance System) portal.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS


What is the purpose and objectives of Food Grievance Portal?
This Portal has been developed to enable consumers to lodge a complaint regarding any issues with Packaged Food, Food Premises, Online Aggregator/ Food Delivery platforms for taking an appropriate action against such complaints and provide timely resolution. The objective of this portal is to enhance consumer experience and build trust around the food available in the market.

What kind of complaints can I submit?

Any complaint relating only to Packaged Food, OR Food Premises, OR Online Aggregator/ Food Delivery platforms can be lodged.

What details do I need to register/ login the system?
You are required to enter your valid email id or mobile number. A One Time Password (OTP) will be sent to the Mobile Number or Email ID that needs to be entered. Once you are successfully registered, you can simply login by selecting login category as ‘Consumer’ and enter your Username and Password
What is the process of registering a complaint?
To register a complaint, a consumer can login into the system and click on the tab ‘Register new complaint’. He can choose from two options – (1) Select from drop down menu and look for the FBO against whom he/she would like to register a complaint amongst the list of 200 FBOs listed already on the platform and send a complaint directly to the nodal officer of the company – (2) If the name of the FBO is not listed in the list of 200 FBOs, he/she can select 🡪 Concern Category from the drop down menu 🡪 Provide Outlet/Premises FSSAI License/Registration No. 🡪 Followed by other details
Is it important to attach a supporting docuement along with the compliant or suggestion?
Yes. An option is available to upload .jpg, .jpeg & .png files are supplementary document you desire along with your complaint/ suggestion. Uploading can be done with click on 'CHOOSE FILE' button while registering your complaint. It is always advisable to attach relevant proofs like image of the product , bill received from the retail shop etc.
Within what time frame my complaint will be resolved?
FSSAI makes sincere efforts to resolve your complaints in the timely manner in collaboration with our State authorities. However, you can also track the status of your complaint online.
Where can I track the complaint already submitted by me?
You can track live status of your complaint by clicking on 'TRACK COMPLAINT' button after login to the system.
What is the process of complaint resolution?
The complaint will be first forwarded to the Packaged food business OR Food premises (restaurant/ QSRs) OR Online Aggregator/ Food Delivery platforms as per the relevant complaint for appropriate resolution with a copy to the State Food Safety Commissioner. For any further feedback/unsatisfactory responses, the complaint will be forwarded to the respective States for subsequent action after a period of 15 days.
Can I edit/modify my complaint or suggestion after it has been submitted?
No. Once submitted, no modifications/change can be made to the complaint already registered. However, a fresh complaint can be submitted by the consumer.
What should I do if the portal stops responding or displays an error?
The possible action could be:
a) Refresh the browser.
b) Log out and login again.
Can complaint be submitted via social media handles of FSSAI or Email provided?
We recommend you to only register your complaint through grievance redressal platform as the same will be proceesed through this portal only.
Is there provision to lodge complaints against your FSOs in the field?
No. Staff Behavior is one of the critical issues and any such incedent(s) can be reported to our Executive Director, HR division with a copy to ceo@fssai.gov.in and State Food Safety Commissioner. Details are available at https://www.fssai.gov.in/cms/commissioners-of-food-safety.php

 

Can I see my history of the lodged complaints?
Yes. You can view all your complaints by clicking on CLOSED COMPLAINTS' button after login to the system.

Is there any provision for feedback about the quality of resolution of my complaint?
Yes, the system gives an option to give the feedback on the resolution of your complaint.

Where can I provide general suggestions to FSSAI?
You can write to us on our social media handles.

MYTH BUSTER

It has been observed time and again that various fake news are circulated through social media channels such as YouTube, WhatsApp, Facebook, etc. which has a wide reach among masses. These fake news are baseless and don't have any scientific facts related to them and due to its presentation, they create a situation of panic among citizens. With all the false and misleading information propagated through these mediums, FSSAI in consultation with the subject experts shares clarification on these fake news. FSSAI also urges citizens to neither believe nor circulate these fake news through their social media channels.

CLARIFICATION ON ICE CREAM AND FROZEN DESSERT

Ice cream and frozen dessert are two standardized products under FSS (FPS and FA) Regulation 2011 and have to follow FSS (Labelling) Regulation along with specific labelling requirements mentioned in the standards itself. These standards/regulations are framed after risk assessment and extensive consultation/deliberation with stakeholders and considering the comments received from the stakeholders on draft notification. By virtue of ingredients including vegetable oil allowed to be used for preparation of frozen desserts as specified in the specific standards, the product is safe for human consumption, if it complies with the requirements of FSSR.

CLARIFICATION ON PACKAGED SALT BEING ADULTERATED WITH DETERGENT

A video surfaced on social media channels, claiming adulteration of salt with detergent. When we completely dissolve any solid substance (solute) in a liquid substance (solvent) it is known as a solution. In this case, salt (solute) dissolves in water (solvent) to form a solution. There is a maximum limit for every solute to dissolve in a fixed amount of solvent, also known as saturation point.

Generally, a maximum of 35 grams of pure salt can be dissolved in 100 ml of water (known as a saturated solution). If more salt is added to this water, it will not get dissolved. Similarly, to dissolve larger amounts of salt, more volume of water is required. E.g.: 500 grams of salt will dissolve in approximately 1.5 litres of water.

In the video, a large amount of salt is being mixed in a small quantity of water, leading to the formation of lumps and foam and being wrongly perceived as adulteration with detergent. Also, a small amount of insoluble particles, silica, phosphates, sulphates, permitted food additives, and in case of Double Fortified Salt (DFS), additionally Encapsulated Ferrous Fumarate (EFF) particles may be visible. These are permissible as per FSS (Food Product Standards and Food Additives) Regulation, 2011 and are not considered adulterants.

CLARIFICATION ON STANDARDS FOR CHOCOLATE, ALLOWING INSECTS

According to FSSAI's standards, any chocolate should be free from insects and other contaminants. Any rumour stating that FSSAI has permitted insects in the chocolate manufacturing process is false.

Regulation 2.7.4 of FSS (Food Product Standards and Food Additives) Regulations 2011 specifies quality standards for chocolate and its types. Further, sub-regulation 3 of this 2.7.4 chocolate standard clearly specifies that the material shall be free from rancidity or off odour, insect and fungus infestation, filth, adulterants and any harmful or injurious matter.

CLARIFICATION REGARDING CONSUMPTION OF POULTRY PRODUCTS AND CORONA VIRUS

The predominant route of transmission of 2019-nCoV (Corona Virus) appears to be human to human as per OIE, though 2019-nCoV may have had an animal source, which requires further investigation. Poultry has not been found to be involved in transmission of 2019-nCoV to humans so far in any report globally. Similarly, outbreaks of Corona Virus in the past (SARS 2002-03, MERS 2012-13) or Corona-associated common cold had no involvement of poultry or poultry products world over.

Thus, with present knowledge of affairs of 2019-nCoV, consumption of poultry and poultry products may be considered safe. General principles of hygiene, however, may be followed as per suggestions of WHO/OIE.

CLARIFICATION REGARDING FERROCYANIDES IN SALT

Ferrocyanides are approved anti-caking agents for common salt including edible common salt, iron fortified salt and iodized salt as per FSSAI Regulations. In India it is allowed at a level of 10 mg/kg compared to EU regulations where it is allowed 20 mg/kg of salt.

With regard to the apt level of exposure of cyanide from such substances, recently one of the highly respected bodies in the world of science, the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA), has opined that the acceptable daily intake of sodium/potassium/calcium ferrocyanides is 0.03 mg per kg body weight per day expressed as ferrocyanide ion.

CLARIFICATION ABOUT PLASTIC EGGS

There is no technology or chemicals available to complete the manufacturing of whole eggs. Also, it is not an economically viable process since normal eggs are available in abundance and at an affordable price.

A thick eggshell membrane indicates the freshness of the eggs. When the egg is fresh, there is not enough time for evaporation to separate the two membranes (outer and inner layers), causing them to stick to each other. This makes it look thicker and stronger when it is peeled. Also, the inner shell membrane of the eggs may become harder and elastic when it is older than 15 days, which is a natural ageing process.

The reason why eggs differ from each other is because of a difference in quality of feed, breed of bird, ageing of eggs and the handling of eggs. As the egg ages, the egg white changes its consistency, becomes thin and runny and finally egg yolk and white dissolve into each other, and this mixing is aggravated by temperature abuse and not because the egg is made artificially.

CLARIFICATION REGARDING MELAMINE IN INFANT MILK

Melamine has no known food uses. Melamine could be present in a food either on account of its incidental presence (as a contaminant) or due to deliberate addition (as an adulterant) for economic gains.

It must be noted that the use of melamine either as an ingredient or as an additive is not permitted in any food by FSSAI, although maximum limits for presence of melamine in food, including milk, have been prescribed to address the incidental presence of melamine as a contaminant. This is as per international standards and is based on a proper risk assessment with respect to consumer health safety. Setting up of such limits certainly does not mean that FSSAI allows addition of melamine to foods even within the specified maximum limit.

CLARIFICATION REGARDING PRESENCE OF PLASTIC IN WHEAT FLOUR

Wheat flour naturally contains two proteins – Glutenin (for elasticity) and Gliadin (for raising of dough). In the presence of water, these two proteins bond together to create an elastic network of protein called gluten strings. Gluten is the rubbery mass that is left when wheat flour dough is washed with water, and this is maliciously referred to as "plastic".

CLARIFICATION REGARDING PRESENCE OF PLASTIC RICE IN MARKET

It is a natural phenomenon of rice to burn since it is a complex carbohydrate, and since rice is 80% starch, it has cohesive and adhesive properties. When the rice is cooked and transformed into a ball, air gets entrapped and it becomes bouncy like a ball. Thus, it should be ruled out that the rice contains plastic.

Video regarding Plastic Rice
CLARIFICATION REGARDING PRESENCE OF PLASTIC IN SNACKS

Snacks such as namkeens and potato chips are mainly made of cereals, spices and oil, and carbohydrates and fats have a natural characteristic of burning when exposed to fire. Thus, it should be ruled out that they contain plastic.
